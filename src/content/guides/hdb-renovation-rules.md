HDB controls renovation in its flats more closely than any other kind of home in Singapore. Three rules matter most: use a contractor from HDB’s directory, get a permit before the works that need one, and don’t touch what HDB doesn’t allow at all.

## Use a contractor from HDB’s directory

The Housing & Development (Renovation Control) Rules 2006 require you to hire contractors only from HDB’s [Directory of Renovation Contractors](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/looking-for-renovation-contractors) (DRC). That applies to every job in the flat, including works that don’t need a permit. You can search the directory, and the list of BCA Approved Window Contractors, with HDB’s e-service.

Being on the directory isn’t a seal of quality: HDB says it doesn’t endorse or guarantee their work. It tells you who may work in your flat. How to judge the rest is in [checking a contractor before you sign](/hiring/checking-a-contractor/).

You remain responsible for the renovation. HDB says the owner has to make sure the contractor follows its guidelines, and the owner can be prosecuted for unauthorised work.

## How the permit works

1. **You authorise your contractor.** They submit the application online through HDB’s APEX system, with an acknowledgement form you’ve signed. HDB sends you an SMS when it receives the application.
2. **A floor plan goes in with it**, marked with the proposed works. You can use the plan from your sales brochure.
3. **For a new flat, open your utilities account first.** HDB won’t grant a permit until the flat has a water and electricity account with SP Services.
4. **Start only once you have the permit.** You can check it with HDB’s Check Renovation Permit e-service.
5. **Finish within the permit period.** HDB gives 3 months from the permit date for flats in new blocks, 1 month in existing blocks, and 2 weeks for window works alone.
6. **Any change needs a new application**, whether you add works or change contractor.

Other trades have their own licensing. HDB lists a PUB Licensed Plumber for water and sanitary works, a Licensed Gas Service Worker (or City Energy) for gas pipes, and an EMA-licensed electrical worker for wiring. [Who’s allowed to do electrical, gas, plumbing and window work](/rules-and-permits/licensed-trades/) has the details.

## What needs a permit, and what doesn’t

These examples come from HDB’s renovation guidelines. Works that don’t need a permit must still follow HDB’s rules, or they count as unauthorised. Anything not covered in the guidelines, or with different measurements, needs HDB’s approval before it starts.

| Work | Permit? |
|---|---|
| Hacking or demolishing any wall, partly or completely | Yes. Non-load-bearing concrete walls also need a Professional Engineer to supervise |
| Taking down a lightweight internal wall (block, brick, drywall or Ferrolite) | Yes |
| Replacing floor finishes (ceramic, homogeneous, marble, timber and others) | Yes |
| Replacing wall finishes up to the ceiling | Yes |
| Raising the floor with lightweight screed at the balcony, kitchen or bathroom | Yes |
| Replacing any window | Yes, by a BCA Approved Window Contractor |
| Replacing a fire-rated main door | Yes. The new door must be half-hour fire-rated, with a self-closing frame |
| Moving an internal doorway | Yes, and never through a concrete wall |
| Laying vinyl, carpet or linoleum | No |
| Laying new floor finishes over the old ones with adhesive (13mm or less in all) | No |
| Building a 63mm hollow-block wall, glass-block wall or gypsum partition | No |
| A false ceiling (at least 2.4m above the floor outside bathrooms, non-combustible) | No |
| Replacing the main gate with one of the same width, panels and swing | No |
| Most extra lighting points, fan points and 13A sockets | No, but an EMA-licensed electrical worker must do the wiring |

## What HDB doesn’t allow at all

HDB lists works it won’t approve because they overload the structure, change the look of the block, create a nuisance or fire risk, or intrude into public space. They include:

- hacking or removing structural parts: reinforced concrete walls, columns, beams, slabs, and the staircase in an executive maisonette;
- loading the floor with more than 150kg per square metre;
- plastering ceilings;
- partitions made of combustible or toxic materials, such as plywood, plastics or asbestos;
- raising the floor with concrete by more than 50mm, including the finishes;
- painting outside the flat, such as common corridor walls and ceilings, or fixing awnings or other fixtures outside it;
- moving or enlarging the refuse chute opening;
- enclosing the aircon ledge or putting grilles on it, and enclosing open balconies or loft terraces;
- casement windows on a façade that faces the common corridor.

## New flats: the 3-year bathroom rule

In a newly built flat, the bathroom and toilet floor and wall finishes HDB laid can’t be replaced for **3 years from the date the block was completed**. Note that HDB counts from the block’s completion, not from your key collection. The rule protects the waterproofing membrane under the tiles. During those 3 years you can still lay new floor tiles over the old ones using adhesive.

After the 3 years, replacing bathroom floor finishes needs a permit, a waterproofing screed and a membrane turned up at least 150mm against the walls, and you and your contractor must do a water test when it’s done. More in the [bathroom guide](/room-by-room/bathroom-renovation/).

## If the rules are broken

- Hiring a contractor who isn’t on the directory, or breaking the renovation rules, can lead to a fine of up to S$5,000 on conviction, for owners and contractors alike.
- Work done without a required permit is unauthorised. HDB can tell you to reinstate the flat to its original condition, and if it lets the work stay, you pay an administrative fee.
- HDB can revoke a permit if its guidelines aren’t followed.

Before the works start, you also have to give your neighbours written notice and keep to HDB’s working hours: see [renovation working hours and your neighbours](/during-the-works/renovation-working-hours/).
