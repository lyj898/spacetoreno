Every renovation is different, but the order of the work is surprisingly constant. Getting the order right saves money: tiles laid before a pipe is moved get hacked up again, and cabinets measured before the walls are finished don’t fit.

## 1. Plan and budget

Decide what you want to change, room by room, and what you’ll keep. For a new flat, start by inspecting it: HDB asks owners of new flats to report defects before renovation starts, while they’re easy to see. Then set a budget with a contingency in it. See [budgeting for a renovation](/planning-and-budget/renovation-budget/).

Find out early what your home’s rules allow: [which works need approval](/rules-and-permits/what-needs-approval/).

## 2. Hire a designer or contractor

Decide whether you need an [interior designer or a contractor](/hiring/interior-designer-or-contractor/), [check the firms](/hiring/checking-a-contractor/), and [compare quotes like for like](/hiring/comparing-renovation-quotes/). The contract should include a work schedule with a start date and completion date, agreed by both of you, and a payment schedule that follows it.

## 3. Get the approvals

Nothing that needs a permit starts before the permit is granted:

- **HDB flat**: your HDB-registered contractor applies for the permit on your behalf. HDB only grants it for a new flat once the utilities account is open.
- **Condo**: apply to the MCST under its by-laws, and to BCA through a Professional Engineer if anything structural is involved.
- **Landed house**: URA and BCA approval through a Qualified Person if you’re adding floor area or changing the structure.

Neighbours come next. HDB flat owners must give written notice to neighbours within two units at least 5 days before works start. See [working hours and neighbours](/during-the-works/renovation-working-hours/).

## 4. Hacking and wet works

The noisy, dusty part comes first:

- **protection** of floors, lifts and common areas;
- **hacking**: walls, old tiles, built-in carpentry;
- **gas, plumbing and electrical** rough-ins, by licensed workers (City Energy asks for the gas supply to be cut off and the meter removed during renovation);
- **wet works**: screeding, waterproofing, tiling and plastering.

In HDB flats this is also when the strictest hours apply: noisy works only on weekdays, 9am to 5pm. Debris has to leave properly: see [hacking and debris](/during-the-works/hacking-and-debris/). If you’re clearing out the home first, do it before this stage starts.

## 5. Carpentry, fittings and painting

Once the wet trades are done and dry:

- **false ceilings** and lighting;
- **carpentry** measured on site and installed: kitchen cabinets, wardrobes, storage;
- **fittings**: sanitary ware, taps, lights, switches and sockets;
- **painting**, and the electrical testing your licensed electrical worker arranges.

## 6. Handover and defects

Walk through every room with your contractor, list what needs fixing, and hold back the final payment until it’s done. [How renovation payments are staged](/hiring/renovation-payment-schedule/) explains why. Collect your certificates and warranties.

What comes after the works (the post-renovation clean, checking every trade’s work, and moving in) is covered in OurKampung’s [after-renovation checklist](https://ourkampung.com/new-home/after-renovation-checklist/), our sister guide to running your home.
