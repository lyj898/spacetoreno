Bedrooms and living areas are where layouts change and finishes get chosen. The rules are mostly about the structure you can’t touch, the weight you put on it, and who wires the power.

## Walls

**HDB flats**

- All hacking and demolition of walls, partial or complete, needs **HDB’s prior written approval**. Lightweight block, brick, drywall and Ferrolite walls can come down with a permit, working from the top down and only up to the underside of the beam or slab above.
- Taking down a non-load-bearing **reinforced concrete** wall also needs a Professional Engineer to supervise. **Structural** walls, columns, beams and slabs can’t be removed at all.
- New walls of 63mm hollow block, 80mm glass block or gypsum board need **no permit**. Partitions of combustible or toxic materials, such as plywood or plastics, aren’t allowed.
- Wall fixings: screws, nails and bolts no more than **6mm** in diameter and **40mm** deep. Ferrolite walls take up to **50kg per bolt**.

**Condos and landed homes**: demolishing a wall that carries only its own weight is an insignificant building work, with no plans to BCA, though a condo’s by-laws may require approval. Structural walls need a Professional Engineer and BCA approval. URA needs no planning permission to enlarge rooms in a landed house.

## Floors

**HDB flats**

- Replacing floor finishes (tiles, marble, timber and others) needs a **permit**.
- Laying **vinyl, carpet or linoleum**, or new finishes over the existing floor with adhesive, needs **no permit**, as long as the total thickness including adhesive is no more than 13mm.
- Timber or laminate laid over existing finishes must not form a raised platform or use cement screed, and the old finishes can’t be hacked for it.
- Where floors are replaced, finishes plus screed can be no more than **50mm**, using pre-packed screed. The floor slab can’t be loaded with more than 150kg per square metre.

Elsewhere, replacing floor finishes is an insignificant building work for BCA; check your condo by-laws.

## Ceilings

In HDB flats, a **false ceiling** needs no permit but must be non-combustible and at least **2.4m** above the finished floor (outside bathrooms). Plastering ceilings isn’t allowed, and gas pipes must never be hidden in a false ceiling. Lightweight false ceilings are on BCA’s insignificant-works list, with conditions.

## Power and lighting

- All wiring must be done by an **EMA-licensed electrical worker**. In HDB flats, most extra lighting points, fan points and 13A sockets need no permit.
- Since 1 July 2023, homes must be protected by **30mA RCCBs** on socket and lighting circuits. If your board is older, ask your electrical worker about it.

## Air-conditioning

- Units outside the building must be installed by a **BCA-trained installer**, on a stainless steel bracket or a purpose-designed concrete ledge. BCA asks owners to check the supports at least every 6 months.
- In HDB flats: condensing units can’t go along the common corridor, the ledge is for the condensing unit only, piping must run inside the flat, no holes may be hacked through concrete for it, and an installation report goes to your HDB branch within 14 days. A new 15A power point for an aircon needs a permit in flats without higher electrical loading.

## Gas and bedrooms

In HDB flats, a space with gas pipes can’t be converted into a bedroom, and gas appliances aren’t allowed in sleeping areas: worth knowing before you move walls around the kitchen.
