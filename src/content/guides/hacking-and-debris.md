Hacking produces a lot of heavy, dusty waste in a short time, and the rules on where it goes are strict. Agree in the contract who removes it, how often, and where it goes.

## Where debris can and can’t go

**Never:**

- down the **refuse chute**, the toilet or a floor trap; HDB charges the owner for any choke or damage;
- into the sewers, onto staircases or passageways, or into the pneumatic waste system;
- into **household bins or recycling bins**: NEA classes renovation waste as industrial waste, to be sent to a licensed recycling facility, not mixed with general waste or recyclables;
- into the town council’s **free bulky-item service**, which excludes renovation debris.

**Instead:**

- In HDB estates where the **Town Council** removes debris, pack it neatly at the Town Council’s designated site, and pay for the haulage and removal.
- Where there’s no Town Council service, HDB says the owner must make sure the contractor takes the debris to an **NEA-approved site**. In 2023 the Ministry of National Development said contractors are required to do this at the end of every workday.
- Anyone collecting waste as a business needs an **NEA General Waste Collector’s Licence**, and construction and renovation debris is one of the kinds of waste licensed collectors handle.

## Skip tanks

A skip tank in a common area of an HDB estate needs the Town Council’s approval, and one in an HDB car park needs an HDB permit. In a condo, ask the management office where debris may be stored and when it can be moved.

## The penalties

- Illegal dumping is an offence under the Environmental Public Health Act. NEA’s most recent statement gives a first offender a fine of up to S$50,000, up to 12 months in jail, or both.
- In 2023, MND said HDB penalises contractors who dump debris on common property with S$500 and 6 demerit points; 24 points in 24 months bars a contractor from new HDB jobs for 12 months.

## Asbestos is different

If an older home has asbestos in its ceiling boards, partitions or roof, it must be removed by an MOM-approved asbestos-removal contractor, and the waste goes to a licensed toxic industrial waste collector, not with the rest of the debris. See [licensed trades](/rules-and-permits/licensed-trades/).
