The short answer: **hire an interior designer if you want someone to design the space and run the project; hire a contractor if you already know what you want built.** Either way, check the same things before you sign.

## What each one does

**An interior designer (ID)** works out the layout, the look, the materials and the carpentry with you, produces drawings, and then manages the builders: hacking, tiling, carpentry, electrical, plumbing and painting. You deal with one firm from first sketch to handover.

**A renovation contractor** carries out the building work. Some offer simple design help, but in general you bring the decisions: what goes where, which tiles, how the cabinets look. You’re closer to the work and make more of the calls yourself.

In law the line between them is thin. The Competition and Consumer Commission’s renovation guide uses “contractor” to mean interior designers and renovation contractors alike, and the same fair-trading rules apply to both. We found no licence specific to interior designers.

## What every route needs

- **In an HDB flat, the firm doing the work must be on HDB’s [Directory of Renovation Contractors](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/business-partners/renovation-contractors/directory-of-renovation-contractors-drc).** It’s a legal requirement, under the Housing and Development (Renovation Control) Rules, and you can be prosecuted for using someone who isn’t. If an ID firm will use other contractors, ask who holds the HDB registration and applies for your permit.
- **Structural work needs a Professional Engineer**, and for landed homes a Qualified Person to submit plans. Neither an ID nor a contractor can stand in for one. See [which works need approval](/rules-and-permits/what-needs-approval/).
- **Licensed trades** still apply: approved window contractors, licensed electrical workers, licensed plumbers and gas workers. See [licensed trades](/rules-and-permits/licensed-trades/).

## How to choose

| You’re likely to want an interior designer if… | You’re likely to want a contractor if… |
|---|---|
| You’re changing the layout or doing the whole home | The job is contained: a bathroom, new flooring, painting |
| You want help choosing materials, colours and carpentry | You know what you want, or are happy to decide on site |
| You don’t have time to coordinate the trades | You can be there to answer questions and check progress |
| You want one firm answerable for design and build | You’d rather pay only for the building work |

Some points apply whoever you pick:

- **Ask for an itemised quote.** The regulator’s guide says quotes should be transparent, accurate, clear and itemised, with any mandatory charges stated upfront. With an ID, that includes the design fee. See [comparing renovation quotes](/hiring/comparing-renovation-quotes/).
- **Check the firm.** HDB registration, its business record and any complaints: see [checking a contractor before you sign](/hiring/checking-a-contractor/).
- **Consider CaseTrust accreditation.** CaseTrust-accredited renovation businesses, ID firms among them, must protect your deposit with a performance bond, use CASE’s standard renovation contract and give a 12-month workmanship warranty.
