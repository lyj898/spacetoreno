A quote is a description of the work with a price attached. Comparing totals without reading the description is how people end up paying for the same renovation twice: once in the contract, and again in “extras”.

## Give everyone the same brief

Write down what you want before you ask for quotes, and send the same brief to everyone:

- the property: type, age, size, and whether it’s a new or resale flat;
- room by room: what’s being hacked, built, replaced or kept;
- finishes you’ve chosen, or a range you’re happy with;
- carpentry: what, where, and roughly how big;
- electrical and lighting points, and any plumbing or gas changes;
- when you need the works done.

If a firm suggests something different, ask them to price your brief and show the alternative separately.

## What an itemised quote looks like

The Competition and Consumer Commission’s guide to the renovation industry says quoted prices should be **transparent, accurate, clear and itemised**, with mandatory charges stated upfront and anything that can’t be calculated in advance disclosed and estimated. It also expects a reasonably detailed description of the goods and services in the contract. In practice, each line should tell you:

- **what**: the item, with brand, model or material where it matters;
- **how much**: a quantity and a unit (an area, a length, or a number of items);
- **where**: the room or wall;
- **the price** for that line.

“Kitchen carpentry, lump sum” doesn’t let you compare anything. Ask for it broken down.

## The lines that go missing

These are often left out of a cheaper quote, or priced as extras later. Ask each firm whether they’re included:

- **Approvals and permits**: who applies, and whether any Professional Engineer’s fee is included where one is needed.
- **Protection** of floors, lifts and common areas, as your HDB rules or condo by-laws require.
- **Hacking and debris disposal**, including how often debris is taken away. In HDB estates it has to go to Town Council sites or NEA-approved sites, never down the refuse chute.
- **Making good** after hacking: plastering, screeding and waterproofing, not just the new tiles.
- **Electrical**: the number of points, and whether new wiring and the licensed electrical worker’s certification are included.
- **Final cleaning**: a builder’s clean is not a post-renovation clean.
- **Variations**: how changes are priced once work starts.

## Comparing them

Line the quotes up in a simple table, one row per item, one column per firm. Where one firm has a line the others don’t, ask the others whether it’s included somewhere else or missing. Then compare:

1. **Scope**: is everything in your brief priced, by everyone?
2. **Specification**: same thickness of board, same tile range, same fittings?
3. **Exclusions**: what will you pay for separately?
4. **Payment terms**: how much upfront, and what triggers each payment? See [how renovation payments are staged](/hiring/renovation-payment-schedule/).
5. **Warranty and schedule**: how long, and when will the works start and finish?

Only then compare totals. If one quote is far below the rest, the usual reason is missing scope, not a better deal.

## Before you accept

- Don’t be rushed: taking advantage of a consumer by putting undue pressure on them to sign is an unfair practice under the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act.
- Make sure the final quote becomes part of the contract. See [what your renovation contract should say](/hiring/renovation-contract/).
