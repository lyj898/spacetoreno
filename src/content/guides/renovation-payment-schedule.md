The safest renovation payment schedule follows the work: each payment comes due when you can see the stage it pays for. Then if a firm stops work or closes, you’ve lost as little as possible.

## Why it matters

Renovation is where Singapore consumers lose the most money paid in advance. CASE reported that renovation accounted for about S$728,000 in prepayment losses in 2024, the highest of any sector, and it received 962 complaints against renovation contractors that year.

## What CASE suggests

CASE’s tips for prepayment protection suggest asking for:

- a **10% deposit** when you sign the contract;
- **80% in stages**, as each step of the works is completed;
- the **final 10% 14 days after satisfactory completion** of all works.

This is advice, not law. Your contract sets the actual amounts, and the consumer regulator’s brochure says that if you pay progressively, the payment schedule should line up with the agreed work schedule.

## How CaseTrust protects deposits

If the firm is CaseTrust-accredited, the scheme’s rules give you more protection:

- the **initial deposit is capped at 20%** of the total cost;
- payments are collected in phases at agreed milestones;
- the firm must buy a **deposit performance bond** that protects your deposit if it closes, is wound up or goes into liquidation;
- after paying, you receive proof of protection showing your details and the protected sum.

Check accreditation on CaseTrust’s own list: see [checking a contractor](/hiring/checking-a-contractor/).

## Good habits for every payment

- **Tie each payment to a stage you can check**, such as hacking finished, tiling done or carpentry installed, not to dates alone.
- **Don’t pay for materials far in advance** unless they’re made to order, and then ask for the supplier’s invoice.
- **Get a receipt every time**, naming the stage the payment covers.
- **Hold back the last payment** until the defects you’ve listed are fixed.
- **Pay in a way you can trace.** CASE notes that credit card payments may be recoverable through a chargeback if the service isn’t delivered.
- **Agree variations in writing first**, with their price, so they don’t turn up as a surprise on the next invoice. See [what your renovation contract should say](/hiring/renovation-contract/).

## Renovation loans

Banks offer renovation loans. Under MAS Notice 635, a bank renovation loan is excluded from the S$20,000 minimum annual income rule for other unsecured bank credit only if the money is used for renovation, it’s repaid within 5 years, and the borrower’s renovation loans from that bank total no more than **6 months’ income or S$30,000, whichever is lower**. Interest rates and fees vary between banks, so compare them as you would any loan. Plan the budget first: see [budgeting for a renovation](/planning-and-budget/renovation-budget/).
